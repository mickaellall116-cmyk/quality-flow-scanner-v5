# Sources: firm organization, overfitting defenses, research-to-production — index, 2026-10-07

A. Pod shops / multi-manager platforms:
- Morgan Stanley ("How Multi-Manager Platforms Find Strength in Numbers", morganstanley.com): centralized overlays hedge unwanted factor risk; per-PM limits on target dollar volatility, volatility-based drawdown triggers, gross/net dollar exposure, position concentration as % of trading levels; monitor overlaps in country/industry/style factors and "crowded" trades; quant strategies diversified across horizons with dollar-volume and VaR limits; live oversight of risk budgets with real-time feedback + at-the-touch liquidity.
- quantlabsnet post: at major multi-strat funds, drawdown limits (often 5–7% of allocated capital) → book automatically liquidated by central risk desk, PM often removed. Surviving PMs target Sharpe > 2.5 to use leverage safely, run dozens of models, manage cross-model correlation.
- Medium "Death of the Single-Manager Hedge Fund" (Gregory Blotnick): multi-PM platforms ≈$931B AUM by Q1 2025 (~1/4 of industry); 10y to Mar 2024 composite 7.38% annual vs 4.93% traditional, ~half volatility; correlation to S&P 500 of 0.17 vs 0.52.

B. AQR / Asness on hypothesis testing:
- Institutional Investor ("Cliff Asness Has Steered Hedge Fund AQR Through Not One, Not Two, But Three Quant Crises"): AQR "went through hundreds of hypotheses generated by the outside" for why value was suffering — rigorous analysis; found no reason to abandon; Asness: "We got way too big... said yes to everything" pre-2018; firm shrank ~40% of ~1,000 staff. (Interview-based, primary-ish quotes from Asness.)
- ai-cio.com (Morningstar conference 2019): Asness "tests for every possibility of why now might be different"; worries factors get arbitraged away with too much money; monitors crowding.
- mathinvestor.org: de Prado was principal/head of ML at AQR — his research program ran inside AQR's strategy development.

C. Renaissance (see notes/source-zuckerman-rentech.md for detail). Additionally: Reuters via QuantNet (2006, Simons IAFE keynote): hires only scientists (physicists, mathematicians, astronomers, computer scientists), "typically know nothing about finance"; strict academic rigor.

D. Research-to-production / incubation:
- QuantifiedStrategies.com ("Does Quant Trading Work?"): throw away ~29 of 30 ideas; of the 1 in 30 pursued, ~4 in 10 go live; recommends demo-account incubation ≥ 6 months; "most trading strategies fail during the incubation period"; ~60% don't survive the six-month incubation.
- fabian-n8n/ai-quant: realistic timeline 6–12 months paper trading at that holding period; validation checklist (30–50 closed trades min, walk-forward only, beat SPY on return+Sharpe net of costs, beat 200-DMA + random entry, positive expectancy, log all variants).
- ivrwealth-ui/trading-library (10-backtest-to-live.md): live protocol — paper trade weeks→months (execution/cost/data gaps surface cheaply); go live small; reconcile live vs backtest predicted performance for same period (persistent gap = modeling error); monitor for decay; pre-commit retirement conditions (DD threshold, tracking-error threshold, regime change). "The strategy is the least important part... the discipline is what determines" outcomes.
- Alpha decay (DWongResearch, medium): 3 reasons — (1) competition for the same edge (crowding), (2) regime change, (3) capacity constraints. Alpha decay ≠ backtest overfitting.
- Execution cost modeling: Almgren–Chriss (Almgren & Chriss 2000) — optimal execution framework: minimize E[cost]+λVar[cost]; permanent vs temporary impact; industry standard for pre-trade analytics and TCA conditioning (dev.cube.exchange explainer; wikipedia model page). Implementation shortfall = slippage from arrival price.

E. Overfitting defenses in practice (assembled):
- Haircuts: industry 50% rule → Harvey–Liu principled (nonlinear; see source-harvey-liu-haircuts.md).
- MinTRL/MinBTL: Bailey & López de Prado (2012) "The Sharpe Ratio Efficient Frontier", J. Risk 15(2): minimum track-record length before observed Sharpe is statistically significant; scales with trials.
- Strategy correlation limits: pod shops monitor inter-manager correlation and crowded trades (Morgan Stanley); ensemble diversification across uncorrelated models (quantlabsnet).
- Limiting researcher degrees of freedom: Renaissance anti-silo + shared incentives as org solution; de Prado's strategy factory specialization (Data Curators / Feature Analysts / Strategists / Backtesting Team / Deployment Team / PMs) as industrialization; log all trials (honest N) for DSR.
- Ensemble: multiple weakly-correlated strategies diversified across horizons (pod shops); de Prado AFML meta-labeling separates side vs size.
