## Ranked shortlist

### 1. Dividend month premium — returns are high in the month a dividend is predicted
- **Claim:** Stocks earn positive abnormal returns in months when a dividend is predicted from their own past payment schedule (~53 bps/month vs all other stocks; ~37 bps/month vs dividend payers in non-predicted months) — price pressure from dividend-seeking investors, not news about the dividend.
- **Source:** Hartzmark & Solomon, "The Dividend Month Premium," *Journal of Finance* 68 (2013) (SSRN working paper, posted 2011, rev. 2014).
- **Source URL:** https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=1930620
- **Timeframe:** Monthly formation/hold — predicted-dividend status for month t is formed from payments 3/6/9/12 months earlier; daily returns inside the month are part of the published description. Monthly lane, daily data.
- **Mechanically definable:** Yes — predicted payer = paid a dividend 3, 6, 9 or 12 months ago on the stock's established schedule, using only payments already made before month t; long-only form = hold predicted payers for that month. No discretion.
- **PIT data:** Yes — inputs are past dividend payment/ex-dates and daily closes, both known at formation; the prediction rule never uses the current month's actual dividend, so there is no look-ahead by construction. Name the inputs: historical dividend calendar + daily OHLCV (already handled).
- **Strategy family:** `dividend_month_premium`
- **Why testable:** Runs entirely on data we already handle, on the frozen universe; monthly turnover is cheap. Distinct from Harvest #6's dividend *initiation* drift (a first-ever dividend announcement event) — this is a recurring-schedule calendar effect on established payers. Frozen gate sketch: forward return of predicted-payer group vs non-predicted payers at 25 bps, with the post-ex-day reversal the paper reports measured separately so a gate cannot pass on the run-up and ignore the give-back.
- **Dedupe:** PASS — no registry experiment and no prior harvest sorts on predicted-dividend months; not a re-parameterization of any killed family. Adjacency to Harvest #6 IH03 (dividend initiation) is in mechanism only (recurring schedule vs first announcement) and is flagged here.

### 2. Intraday periodicity — same half-hour returns repeat at daily lags
- **Claim:** A stock's return in a given half-hour interval positively predicts its return in the same half-hour interval on subsequent days (lags of exactly one trading day and multiples), persisting at least 40 trading days; volume and order imbalance show the same pattern but do not explain it.
- **Source:** Heston, Korajczyk & Sadka, "Intraday Patterns in the Cross-Section of Stock Returns," *Journal of Finance* 65 (2010), 1369–1407.
- **Source URL:** https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=1509466&rec=1&srcabs=1461185&pos=1
- **Timeframe:** Intraday — 13 half-hour intervals per trading day; signal = return in interval k on day t, outcome = return in interval k on days t+1 … t+40. Intraday lane (finer than our 4H lane; uses 30-min bars).
- **Mechanically definable:** Yes — interval returns from completed 30-min bars only; rank cross-sectionally within each interval; no discretion once the interval grid (09:30 ET anchor) is frozen.
- **PIT data:** Yes, with a length caveat — inputs are intraday bars, point-in-time by construction (only completed intervals), but the length of clean intraday history we can source is the open data question from earlier harvests, so the testable window may be shorter than the paper's. Demoted below #1 on that partial-data point, per the rubric.
- **Strategy family:** `intraday_periodicity`
- **Why testable:** The menu's only clock-time claim: it is about *when* in the day a stock moves, not about its daily return — genuinely different from Harvest #1's intraday momentum (first half-hour → last half-hour *within* one day) and from ORB (killed beyond its HOLD record; this is no opening-range claim). Parity warning carried from program history: any test must use the frozen 09:30 ET session anchor and pass the candle-parity gate before a run. Frozen gate sketch: same-interval forward return spread at realistic intraday costs — costs are the likely killer and the gate must say so up front; the paper itself frames much of the value as execution timing.
- **Dedupe:** PASS with adjacency flag — family-adjacent to Harvest #1 `intraday_momentum` (different statistic: same-interval across days vs first→last interval within a day) and to the ORB HOLD record (no range-breakout claim). Adjacency costs novelty points; mechanism is different in kind.

### 3. R&D intensity — high R&D relative to market value is underpriced
- **Claim:** Among firms doing R&D, high R&D intensity (R&D relative to equity market value) predicts higher subsequent returns — strongest in growth stocks and in high-R&D/market-value firms with poor past returns; the market gives insufficient credit for R&D investment.
- **Source:** Chan, Lakonishok & Sougiannis, "The Stock Market Valuation of Research and Development Expenditures," *Journal of Finance* 56 (2001), 2431–2456 (NBER WP 7223, 1999).
- **Source URL:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w7223
- **Timeframe:** Annual formation (R&D from the filed annual report), monthly/annual hold. Annual-formation lane on daily/monthly returns.
- **Mechanically definable:** Yes — R&D intensity = reported R&D expense scaled by market value of equity at formation; rank cross-sectionally; no discretion once the scaling and the growth-stock conditioning are frozen.
- **PIT data:** Yes, with discipline — R&D as *filed* in the annual report (EDGAR filed date), never as later restated; formation only after the filing is public, under the same June-lag / filed-date discipline as Harvests #4–#6 fundamental candidates. Inputs: EDGAR annual R&D + market value at formation.
- **Strategy family:** `rnd_intensity`
- **Why testable:** One free public route (SEC EDGAR) already scoped by earlier harvests; annual turnover is cheap. Not a VCP claim (no price pattern, no quality screen) and not gross profitability (an expensed intangible input, not a margin). Frozen gate sketch: forward return of high- vs low-R&D-intensity groups at 25 bps, reported separately inside/outside growth stocks, because the paper's claim is conditional — an unconditional pass or fail alone would not settle it.
- **Dedupe:** PASS — no registry experiment or prior harvest sorts on R&D intensity; distinct from Harvest #7-adjacent innovation efficiency (#6 below), which divides patent *output* by R&D rather than pricing R&D input itself.

### 4. Operating leverage — high operating costs relative to assets earn higher returns
- **Claim:** Firms with high operating leverage (operating costs levering the exposure of assets in place) earn significantly higher average returns; sorts on operating leverage earn significant excess returns, and the effect explains why the value premium is strong within industries but weak across them.
- **Source:** Novy-Marx, "Operating Leverage," *Review of Finance* 15 (2011), 103–134.
- **Source URL:** https://tevgeniou.github.io/EquityRiskFactors/bibliography/OperatingLeverageFactor.pdf
- **Timeframe:** Annual formation (annual operating costs and assets from the filed annual report), monthly/annual hold. Annual-formation lane.
- **Mechanically definable:** Yes — operating leverage from reported annual operating costs scaled by assets (the paper's novel measure), ranked cross-sectionally, with the intra-industry form ranked within industry; no discretion once industry classification is frozen as-of.
- **PIT data:** Yes, with discipline — annual costs/assets as filed (EDGAR filed date / June-lag discipline, as #3). Inputs: EDGAR annual COGS + SG&A and total assets, plus market data for controls.
- **Strategy family:** `operating_leverage`
- **Why testable:** Same EDGAR route as #3 — sequence them, don't build twice. Honest warning carried in the intake: the claim is explicitly a theory of the value premium, so it is family-adjacent to Harvest #6's book-to-market and Harvest #4's gross profitability; a preregistration must control both, or a pass is just value/profitability re-labelled. Recent secondary discussion (fetched this run) argues the unconditional premium may have inverted and that leverage amplifies profitability conditionally — that disagreement is what a frozen gate would settle on our universe. Novelty discounted for the adjacency, per the rubric.
- **Dedupe:** PASS with adjacency flag — no registry experiment sorts on operating costs/assets; not a re-parameterization of book-to-market (different numerator: costs, not book equity) or of gross profitability (cost level, not margin), but the adjacency is real and flagged.

### 5. Labor hiring rate — firms hiring slowly earn higher future returns
- **Claim:** Firms with lower labor hiring rates (and lower investment rates) earn higher future stock returns (~7.05%/yr value-weighted spread for the low-minus-high hiring+investment portfolio), even after controlling for other known predictors; the hiring rate predicts on its own, beyond the investment rate.
- **Source:** Bazdresch, Belo & Lin, "Labor Hiring, Investment and Stock Return Predictability in the Cross Section," working paper (Sep 2009; published version, *Journal of Financial and Quantitative Analysis* 49 (2014), as Belo, Lin & Bazdresch).
- **Source URL:** https://haas.berkeley.edu/wp-content/uploads/Belo_LaborSep2009.pdf
- **Timeframe:** Annual formation (employment change over the filed annual period), annual hold. Annual-formation lane.
- **Mechanically definable:** Yes — hiring rate = change in reported employees scaled by lagged employees; rank cross-sectionally; the testable claim is the hiring leg with the investment rate controlled, not a joint retune of asset growth. No discretion.
- **PIT data:** Yes, with a coverage caveat — employee counts are disclosed in annual filings (EDGAR 10-K, filed-date discipline as #3/#4), but disclosure is sparser and less standardized than financial line items, so coverage on a ~250-name universe must be measured at preregistration and a frozen minimum-coverage rule set before any run. Inputs: EDGAR annual employee counts + asset-growth control.
- **Strategy family:** `labor_hiring_rate`
- **Why testable:** Same EDGAR route as #3/#4. Adjacency flagged: the investment leg overlaps Harvest #4's asset growth — the novel mechanism is the *labor* leg (adjustment costs in hiring proxying time-varying risk), so the preregistration must report the hiring-only sort with asset growth controlled; a pass driven by the investment leg alone teaches nothing new.
- **Dedupe:** PASS with adjacency flag — no registry experiment or prior harvest sorts on employment growth; the hiring mechanism is different in kind from asset growth (balance-sheet expansion), with the overlap controlled as above.

### 6. Innovative efficiency — patents per R&D dollar predict returns
- **Claim:** Innovative efficiency — patents granted (or patent citations) scaled by R&D expenditures — is a strong positive predictor of future returns after controlling for firm characteristics and risk (~38 bps/month value-weighted high-minus-low in the paper); the authors attribute it largely to mispricing / limited attention.
- **Source:** Hirshleifer, Hsu & Li, "Innovative Efficiency and Stock Returns," *Journal of Financial Economics* 107 (2013), 632–654.
- **Source URL:** https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jfinec/v107y2013i3p632-654.html
- **Timeframe:** Annual formation (patents granted in the year, scaled by R&D with the paper's two-year application–grant lag), 12-month hold. Annual-formation lane.
- **Mechanically definable:** Yes — IE = patents granted / lagged cumulative R&D, exactly as defined in the paper; rank cross-sectionally. The firm↔patent-assignee matching rule must be frozen at preregistration (that matching, not the ratio, is the judgment risk).
- **PIT data:** Yes — patent grants are public on their grant date (USPTO) and R&D is as-filed (EDGAR); formation uses only grants and filings public before formation. But this needs a *new* data route (USPTO grants + assignee matching) that none of the earlier candidates need, so lane fit is weakest in the top tier. Inputs: USPTO patent grants/citations, EDGAR R&D, market data.
- **Strategy family:** `innovation_efficiency`
- **Why testable:** The output side of #3: #3 prices the R&D *input*, this prices innovation *output per input dollar* — genuinely different statistics, and they should not be stacked as if independent. Frozen gate sketch: forward return of high- vs low-IE groups at 25 bps among R&D-doing firms only (IE is undefined without R&D), with R&D intensity (#3) controlled.
- **Dedupe:** PASS — no registry experiment or prior harvest uses patent data; distinct from `rnd_intensity` (output/input ratio vs input scaled by market value).

### 7. Option-implied volatility skew (smirk) — steep smirks predict low returns
- **Claim:** Stocks with a steep volatility smirk — implied volatility of out-of-the-money puts minus at-the-money calls — earn lower subsequent returns; in the comparison study fetched this run, implied volatility skew had the most significant predictive power among six option-implied measures across horizons, before and after 2008.
- **Source:** Xing, Zhang & Zhao, "What Does the Individual Option Volatility Smirk Tell Us About Future Equity Returns?" *Journal of Financial and Quantitative Analysis* 45 (2010); comparison evidence: Fu, Arisoy, Shackleton & Umutlu, "Option-Implied Volatility Measures and Stock Return Predictability" (University of Liverpool repository paper, fetched in full this run).
- **Source URL:** https://livrepository.liverpool.ac.uk/3002404/1/FuArisoyShackletonUmutlu2016.pdf
- **Timeframe:** Monthly formation (option implied volatilities on the last trading day of the month, 30–91 days to maturity in the comparison study), 1-week to 3-month holds. Monthly lane.
- **Mechanically definable:** Yes — smirk = IV(OTM put) − IV(ATM call) under frozen moneyness/maturity filters; rank cross-sectionally; no discretion.
- **PIT data:** Unknown — the inputs are historical firm-level option chains as quoted at formation. The papers use OptionMetrics, a paid database we do not have; no free public route to point-in-time historical option chains at length has been established in this program. That fails the rubric's top-tier PIT test, so this candidate is demoted to last despite a novel mechanism — it is shortlisted so the data question is on the record, not because a test is currently buildable.
- **Strategy family:** `option_implied_skewness`
- **Why testable:** Only if a PIT option-chain route is first established (a data question, separately decided — no vendor call is authorized by this harvest). Adjacency flagged: Harvest #5's `option_volatility_spread` is a put–call-parity *deviation* measure; the smirk is a *moneyness slope* — different statistics on the same underlying data route, so the two share the same PIT blocker and should be sequenced together if that route is ever built.
- **Dedupe:** PASS with adjacency flag — no registry experiment sorts on implied-volatility skew; not the parity-deviation claim harvested in #5, but data-route-identical to it.
