## Ranked shortlist

### 1. Liquidity beta — sensitivity to aggregate liquidity innovations (Pástor–Stambaugh)
- **Claim:** Stocks whose returns are more sensitive to fluctuations in aggregate
  market liquidity earn higher average returns — over 1966–1999 the high-sensitivity
  stocks beat the low-sensitivity stocks by 7.5% per year, adjusted for market, size,
  value and momentum exposures.
- **Source:** Pástor & Stambaugh, "Liquidity Risk and Expected Stock Returns,"
  *Journal of Political Economy* 111 (2003), 642–685 (RePEc abstract page fetched here).
- **Source URL:** https://ideas.repec.Org/a/ucp/jpolec/v111y2003i3p642-685.html
- **Timeframe:** Daily data within each month estimate each stock's liquidity
  measure (signed-volume return-reversal coefficient); the aggregate liquidity
  series is monthly; stock liquidity betas are estimated over a trailing window;
  annual formation in the paper's portfolio sorts. Daily lane, monthly/annual formation.
- **Mechanically definable:** Yes — the liquidity measure is a frozen regression
  of next-day excess return on today's return and signed dollar volume; the beta
  is a frozen time-series regression on aggregate-liquidity innovations; no
  discretion once windows are frozen.
- **PIT data:** Yes — daily closes and dollar volume, all completed before
  formation. The aggregate factor is constructed from the same universe's own
  completed data, so there is no external factor-vintage problem, provided the
  prereg constructs each month's aggregate liquidity strictly from data completed
  in that month.
- **Strategy family:** `liquidity_beta`
- **Why testable:** Runs entirely on daily price/volume data we already handle —
  zero new data routes, top testability tier. Adjacency flag (load-bearing):
  Harvest #2's IH02 is the Amihud illiquidity *level* (a stock's own average
  price-impact ratio); this is a *beta* — sensitivity to market-wide liquidity
  innovations — a different estimator and a different mechanism (priced systematic
  liquidity risk vs own-illiquidity compensation). It is also the systematic-risk
  sibling of Harvest #4's aggregate-volatility beta (#2 there): the prereg should
  report the correlation of the two beta sorts, and a pass that is just the Amihud
  level or the volatility beta re-measured is not a new finding.
- **Dedupe:** PASS with adjacency flag (vs Harvest #2 IH02 Amihud level; vs
  Harvest #4 IH02 aggregate-volatility beta) — no registry experiment or prior
  harvest estimates liquidity-innovation betas.

### 2. Net share issuance — firms that issue shares underperform, repurchasers outperform
- **Claim:** Post-1970, share issuance exhibits a strong cross-sectional ability
  to predict stock returns (more significant than size, book-to-market or momentum
  alone): firms that grow their share count underperform, firms that shrink it
  (repurchases) outperform.
- **Source:** Pontiff & Woodgate, "Share Issuance and Cross-sectional Returns,"
  *Journal of Finance* 63 (2008), 921–945 (published-research summary of the
  primary paper fetched here; the entry cites the primary paper per spec — the
  summary itself notes the publisher's full text refuses automated fetch and
  attributes only the abstract claims to the paper).
- **Source URL:** https://www.fidetolabs.com/products/notes/net-share-issuance-holdout
- **Timeframe:** Annual formation (the standard construction forms portfolios
  each June on the change in log split-adjusted shares outstanding from fiscal
  year-end t−2 to t−1); holding periods from months to years in the paper. Daily
  prices, annual formation.
- **Mechanically definable:** Yes — one number per firm-year: the change in log
  split-adjusted shares outstanding; rank; no discretion.
- **PIT data:** Yes, under a discipline that must be frozen at preregistration —
  shares outstanding taken from 10-K/10-Q filings used only after their EDGAR
  filed dates, or under the standard June-lag convention (formation in June of
  year t uses fiscal years ended in t−1 and earlier). Split adjustment must use
  only splits announced/effective before formation. An as-restated-today share
  count pull would fail PIT and is disallowed.
- **Strategy family:** `net_share_issuance`
- **Why testable:** The financing axis — managers issuing into overvaluation /
  repurchasing into undervaluation — a behavioural signal carried in a corporate
  action, not in prices or accounting ratios. Shares the free EDGAR route with
  Harvest #4's #4–#6, so if that plumbing is ever built the marginal cost is
  small. Two adjacency flags: (a) vs Harvest #4's asset growth (#5) — issuers
  are usually also asset-growers, and the later five-factor investment factor
  (CMA) may absorb part of this effect; the prereg must double-sort or control
  for asset growth. (b) vs the registry's VCP records — this is a standalone
  single-measure sort, not a VCP pattern/screen selection claim; flagged for
  human confirmation at preregistration.
- **Dedupe:** PASS with adjacency flags — no registry experiment or prior harvest
  sorts on share-count change.

### 3. Accrual anomaly (Sloan) — cash-backed earnings beat accrual-heavy earnings
- **Claim:** Earnings performance driven by the accrual component is less
  persistent than performance driven by cash flow, and prices act as if investors
  fixate on earnings and miss the difference: the lowest-accrual decile
  outperformed the highest-accrual decile by ~10.4 pp per year (≈9.4 pp
  size-adjusted) in the original 1962–1991 sample.
- **Source:** Sloan, "Do Stock Prices Fully Reflect Information in Accruals and
  Cash Flows About Future Earnings?" *The Accounting Review* 71 (1996), 289–315
  (published-research summary of the primary paper fetched here; the entry cites
  the primary paper per spec).
- **Source URL:** https://multibaggershares.com/sloan-accrual-anomaly-cash-backed-earnings-test-forensic-filter/
- **Timeframe:** Annual accounting data (one fiscal year's accruals, scaled by
  average total assets); one-year hold in the original decile design. Daily
  prices, annual formation.
- **Mechanically definable:** Yes — accruals in either frozen form: the original
  balance-sheet derivation ((ΔCurrent Assets − ΔCash) − (ΔCurrent Liabilities −
  ΔShort-Term Debt − ΔTax Payable) − Depreciation), or the cash-flow form
  (Net Income − Cash Flow from Operations) / Average Total Assets. The prereg
  must pick ONE form and freeze it; the balance-sheet form is the more
  error-prone construction, as noted last run.
- **PIT data:** Yes, under the same frozen discipline as Harvest #4's #4–#5 —
  net income, operating cash flow and total assets from 10-K filings used only
  after their EDGAR filed dates (or a frozen June-lag convention). As-restated
  pulls are disallowed.
- **Strategy family:** `accrual_anomaly`
- **Why testable:** This candidate is promoted, not new: Harvest #4 fetched it,
  passed it at dedupe, and parked it as the stated successor if an EDGAR
  accounting lane is ever built for gross profitability or asset growth — with
  three demotions (third accounting slot in one menu; decay evidence; most
  error-prone construction). It is shortlisted now because this menu has no
  other accounting-axis candidate competing for the slot, and the decay evidence
  is part of the claim to test, not a reason to hide it: Green, Hand & Soliman
  (2011, "Going, Going, Gone?") document that the hedge returns decayed to
  roughly zero as hedge-fund capital crowded in — a modern-sample test on our
  universe is precisely a test of whether anything is left. The prereg must
  state that prior honestly: the expected value here is a clean verdict
  (including an honest NO EDGE), not a presumed live premium.
- **Dedupe:** PASS — novel family; no registry experiment or prior shortlist
  sorts on accruals. Prior status disclosed: passed dedupe in Harvest #4
  (2026-11-02) and was listed there under "not shortlisted — passed dedupe,
  lost on ranking"; it has never been shortlisted or tested.

### 4. Earnings announcement premium — prices rise around scheduled announcement dates
- **Claim:** On average, stock prices rise around scheduled earnings announcement
  dates regardless of the news content: a monthly strategy holding stocks
  expected to announce and shorting those not expected earned ~61 bp per month
  (≈7%/yr, t > 5), with the premium lasting about 20 days, strong in large caps,
  and evident in samples back to 1927.
- **Source:** Frazzini & Lamont, "The Earnings Announcement Premium and Trading
  Volume," NBER Working Paper 13090 (2007) (full working paper fetched here).
- **Source URL:** https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w13090/w13090.pdf
- **Timeframe:** Monthly formation on *expected* announcement month; the premium
  window is the days around the announcement, ≈20 days total. Daily lane.
- **Mechanically definable:** Yes — once the expected-date rule is frozen
  (the paper forecasts this year's announcement date from the prior year's
  actual announcement dates); hold names expected to announce in the coming
  month; no discretion after that.
- **PIT data:** Yes, under the paper's own load-bearing discipline, which the
  prereg must adopt verbatim in spirit: actual announcement dates may NOT be
  used (a delayed announcement itself conveys news, so using the realised date
  is lookahead); only an expected date constructed from information public at
  formation (prior-year announcement pattern) is admissible. Announcement-date
  history is obtainable from public EDGAR filing timestamps (8-K earnings
  releases / 10-Q filings) — free, but a new extraction route, and coverage
  completeness on our 250-name universe must be stated before any return gate.
- **Strategy family:** `earnings_announcement_premium`
- **Why testable:** A calendar/event-timing claim, not a surprise claim — this
  is deliberately NOT post-earnings-announcement drift (PEAD), which sorts on
  the earnings *surprise* and needs analyst-expectation data whose PIT quality
  has repeatedly failed this program's bar (and which is a Track 2 candidate
  under separate review in the Nov 11 comparison). This premium needs only
  *when* a firm is expected to report, never what it reports or what was
  expected of it. Ranked below the price/accounting candidates because the
  expected-date construction is the fragile step: if expected dates cannot be
  built cleanly from past-only data, the candidate dies at preregistration,
  and that is an acceptable outcome.
- **Dedupe:** PASS — no registry experiment or prior harvest trades
  announcement-timing; distinct from PEAD (surprise-sorted drift) in mechanism,
  inputs and counterparty.

### 5. Short interest — heavily shorted stocks underperform (long-only avoidance form)
- **Claim:** Portfolios of stocks with high short interest generally underperform;
  the effect is concentrated in short-sale-constrained stocks (highest short
  interest percentile AND lowest third of institutional ownership), which
  underperformed by 215 bp per month on an equal-weighted basis over 1988–2002 —
  but by only an insignificant 39 bp per month value-weighted.
- **Source:** Asquith, Pathak & Ritter, "Short Interest, Institutional Ownership,
  and Stock Returns," *Journal of Financial Economics* 78 (2005), 243–276
  (published paper fetched here).
- **Source URL:** https://economics.mit.edu/sites/default/files/publications/Short%20Interest%2C%20Institutional%20Ownership.pdf
- **Timeframe:** Monthly short interest observations; forward returns over the
  following month(s), with underperformance reported to persist up to 12 months
  in the earlier literature. Daily prices, monthly formation.
- **Mechanically definable:** Yes — short interest ratio = shares sold short /
  shares outstanding at the published observation; rank; the constrained subset
  adds a frozen institutional-ownership split.
- **PIT data:** Yes, under one non-negotiable rule for the prereg: the signal
  must be keyed to the *dissemination* date of the short interest report
  (FINRA/exchange publication, currently twice monthly), never the settlement
  date the positions refer to — the gap is roughly two weeks, and keying to
  settlement date would credit the strategy with information the public did
  not yet have. Short interest data are public and free via FINRA/exchange
  publication; institutional ownership (13F) is public via EDGAR but arrives
  with its own filing lag, which must also be keyed to filed dates.
- **Strategy family:** `short_interest`
- **Why testable:** A positioning/crowding signal from market-participant
  behaviour — a data type (who is short, and how constrained they are) absent
  from the registry and all prior menus. Two honest caveats, both from the
  paper itself, stated up front: (a) the headline number is equal-weighted and
  concentrated in a tiny constrained subset (~21 stocks per month in the full
  CRSP universe) — on our ~250-name large-cap-tilted universe that subset may
  be nearly empty, so the prereg should first state the cross-sectional spread
  of short interest actually available on this universe; a value-weighted null
  is a live possibility the paper itself reports. (b) The honest tradeable
  form here is long-only avoidance/underweight of heavily shorted names:
  directly shorting the high-short-interest leg as a *daily* system would
  collide with the killed short-side daily family; this candidate is a monthly
  cross-sectional sort, and any short leg belongs only inside a long-short
  research design with costs and borrow stated — flagged for Mike/ChatGPT at
  preregistration, not hidden.
- **Dedupe:** PASS with form restriction flagged (see above) — no registry
  experiment or prior harvest uses short interest or positioning data.

### 6. Distress / failure probability — high-failure-probability stocks earn anomalously low returns
- **Claim:** Firms with high estimated failure probability (dynamic logit on
  accounting and market variables: higher leverage, lower profitability, lower
  market cap, lower past returns, higher volatility, lower cash holdings, higher
  market-to-book, lower price per share) have delivered anomalously low returns
  since 1981, despite higher standard deviations, betas and value/small-cap
  loadings.
- **Source:** Campbell, Hilscher & Szilagyi, "In Search of Distress Risk,"
  *Journal of Finance* 63 (2008), 2899–2947 (published paper fetched here).
- **Source URL:** https://hilscher.ucdavis.edu/CampbellHilscherSzilagyi_JF2008.pdf
- **Timeframe:** Monthly data; failure probability estimated monthly from
  lagged accounting and market variables; monthly formation and hold. Daily lane.
- **Mechanically definable:** Yes, with one freeze that decides honesty: the
  logit coefficients must be frozen *ex ante* — either the paper's published
  coefficients applied as-is, or an expanding-window estimation that at each
  formation date uses only failures realised before that date. Re-estimating
  the model on the full sample and applying it in-sample is lookahead in the
  model itself and is disallowed.
- **PIT data:** Yes, under the EDGAR filed-date / June-lag discipline for the
  accounting inputs (net income, leverage, cash holdings) plus completed market
  data (returns, volatility, market cap, price). Same EDGAR route as candidates
  #2–#3.
- **Strategy family:** `distress_failure_probability`
- **Why testable:** A composite solvency state variable — the claim is that the
  market *overprices* distress (low returns for high risk), the opposite sign
  from a risk-premium story, which makes it a sharp, falsifiable prediction.
  Ranked sixth on testability, not novelty: it is the most model-heavy
  candidate on the menu (a fitted probability, not a single ratio), its inputs
  overlap profitability (#4 in Harvest #4) and volatility families already on
  the menu, and the paper itself shows almost all model variables contribute —
  so a prereg owes overlap reporting against those sorts, and a failure of the
  frozen-coefficient discipline voids the test.
- **Dedupe:** PASS with overlap flags — no registry experiment or prior harvest
  estimates failure probabilities; not a VCP claim (no price pattern, no
  quality screen — the sign of the prediction is opposite to a quality tilt).

### 7. Option-implied volatility spread — deviations from put-call parity predict stock returns
- **Claim:** Using the difference in implied volatility between matched call and
  put options as the deviation from put-call parity: stocks with relatively
  expensive calls outperform stocks with relatively expensive puts by 50 bp
  per week.
- **Source:** Cremers & Weinbaum, "Deviations from Put-Call Parity and Stock
  Return Predictability," *Journal of Financial and Quantitative Analysis* 45
  (2010), 335–367 (RePEc abstract page fetched here).
- **Source URL:** http://ideas.repec.org/a/cup/jfinqa/v45y2010i02p335-367_00.html
- **Timeframe:** Weekly formation and hold (the headline spread is 50 bp per
  week). Weekly lane.
- **Mechanically definable:** Yes — for each stock, the volume-weighted
  call-minus-put implied-volatility spread across matched strike/expiry pairs
  on completed option quotes; rank; no discretion once pair-matching and
  liquidity filters are frozen.
- **PIT data:** Unknown — the inputs are historical end-of-day option chains
  (matched call/put quotes with implied volatilities) timestamped as of each
  formation date. We have no options data route in this program today, and no
  free public source of point-in-time historical option chains at the needed
  depth has been verified in this run. This fails the top-tier testability bar
  (PIT = yes) and is ranked last for exactly that reason.
- **Strategy family:** `option_volatility_spread`
- **Why testable:** The only derivatives-market information claim the harvest
  has produced — informed trading showing up in relative option prices before
  the stock moves, a genuinely different counterparty and data type. Offered
  now, at the bottom, so the menu records it honestly: it becomes testable
  only if a PIT-clean historical options source is ever qualified, and the
  paper itself notes predictability decreased over its sample period.
- **Dedupe:** PASS — no registry experiment or prior harvest uses options data.
