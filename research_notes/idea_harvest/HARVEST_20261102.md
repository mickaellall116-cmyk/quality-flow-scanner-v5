## Ranked shortlist

### 1. Intra-industry lead-lag — big firms lead small firms in the same industry
- **Claim:** Within an industry, the returns of the largest firms predict the
  following weeks' returns of the smallest firms in that same industry, while the
  reverse does not hold — industry information reaches leaders first and diffuses
  slowly to followers, largely through sluggish adjustment to negative news.
- **Source:** Hou, "Industry Information Diffusion and the Lead-Lag Effect in Stock
  Returns," *Review of Financial Studies* 20 (2007), 1113–1138 (Dice Center WP
  2003-23 version fetched here).
- **Source URL:** https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=463005&rec=1&srcabs=2258944&pos=9
- **Timeframe:** Weekly returns (Wednesday-to-Wednesday in the replication
  literature); leader return in week *t* predicts follower returns over the
  following 1–several weeks. Weekly lane.
- **Mechanically definable:** Yes — within each industry, form the large-firm and
  small-firm portfolios by market cap, regress/sort follower returns on completed
  leader returns; no discretion once industry set and size split are frozen.
- **PIT data:** Yes — daily/weekly closes and market caps, all completed before
  formation. One input needs an as-of rule frozen at preregistration: the industry
  classification itself (the paper uses time-varying CRSP SIC codes; a
  today's-classification-applied-retroactively version would be lookahead and is
  disallowed).
- **Strategy family:** `industry_lead_lag_diffusion`
- **Why testable:** The menu's first *cross-stock* predictability claim — the
  signal for a follower is another stock's completed return, not any characteristic
  of the follower itself. Runs on weekly data we already handle. Two adjacency
  flags, both load-bearing: (a) Hou himself shows this lead-lag *drives* the
  industry-momentum anomaly — but industry momentum is rejected at dedupe below
  as a killed momentum retune, so this candidate is admissible ONLY as the
  within-industry leader→follower cross-predictability claim, with the follower's
  own past return controlled; a prereg that cannot separate the two collapses
  into the killed family and must be dropped. (b) Adjacent to Harvest #3's price
  delay (Hou & Moskowitz 2005): delay is a stock's own lagged response to *market*
  returns; this is a *different stock's* return predicting it within an industry —
  different estimator, different mechanism.
- **Dedupe:** PASS with adjacency flags — no registry experiment or prior harvest
  trades cross-autocorrelations between stocks; no killed family matches.

### 2. Aggregate volatility beta — sensitivity to market-volatility innovations
- **Claim:** Stocks with high past sensitivity to innovations in aggregate market
  volatility earn low average returns (the price of aggregate volatility risk is
  ≈ −1% per annum) — they are bid up as hedges against volatility spikes.
- **Source:** Ang, Hodrick, Xing & Zhang, "The Cross-Section of Volatility and
  Expected Returns," *Journal of Finance* 61 (2006), 259–299 (SSRN version
  fetched here).
- **Source URL:** https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=681343&rec=1&srcabs=1787049&pos=2
- **Timeframe:** Daily returns; sensitivity (beta to aggregate-volatility
  innovations, proxied by VIX changes / market-volatility changes) estimated over
  a trailing window, monthly formation and hold. Daily lane.
- **Mechanically definable:** Yes — a frozen time-series regression of each
  stock's daily returns on market-volatility innovations over completed days;
  rank on the estimated beta; no discretion.
- **PIT data:** Yes — daily stock closes plus the daily VIX close (published
  same-day, as-published values), all completed before formation.
- **Strategy family:** `aggregate_volatility_beta`
- **Why testable:** A *systematic* risk-exposure characteristic, mechanically a
  regression beta — a different object from every volatility entry already on the
  menu, which are own-variance forecasts or own-variance levels. Scope discipline
  is the whole game here: this same paper's second finding (high idiosyncratic
  volatility → low returns) is ALREADY Harvest #2's IH01 and is not re-offered;
  this intake is only the aggregate-sensitivity leg, and the prereg must
  double-sort or control for IVOL (and for MAX) or a pass just re-measures
  earlier menu items. Barinov's follow-up (fetched this run under candidate #7)
  makes the same point from the other side: an aggregate-volatility factor
  explains most of the MAX and skewness effects — these candidates are one
  correlated cluster and should be sequenced, not stacked.
- **Dedupe:** PASS with overlap flag (vs Harvest #2 IH01, a different leg of the
  same paper) — no registry experiment estimates volatility-innovation betas.

### 3. Tail-risk beta — loadings on the common tail-risk factor (Kelly–Jiang)
- **Claim:** A time-varying market tail-risk measure can be estimated each month
  from the cross-section of individual stock crashes (a pooled tail exponent);
  stocks with high loadings on past tail risk earn an annual three-factor alpha
  5.4% higher than stocks with low loadings.
- **Source:** Kelly & Jiang, "Tail Risk and Asset Prices," *Review of Financial
  Studies* 27 (2014), 2841–2871 (Chicago Booth RP 13-67 / SSRN version fetched
  here).
- **Source URL:** https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2321243
- **Timeframe:** Daily returns pooled within each month to estimate that month's
  tail exponent; stock tail loadings estimated over a trailing window; monthly
  formation and hold. Daily lane.
- **Mechanically definable:** Yes — the tail measure is a frozen Hill-estimator
  recipe on completed daily returns below a frozen threshold, and loadings are a
  frozen regression; no discretion once threshold and windows are frozen.
- **PIT data:** Yes — daily closes only. The tail factor is constructed from the
  same universe's own completed returns, so there is no external factor-vintage
  problem — provided the prereg constructs the factor strictly from past data at
  each formation date.
- **Strategy family:** `tail_risk_beta`
- **Why testable:** A crash-state exposure, distinct from candidate #2's
  volatility-level innovations (tails vs variance) and from Harvest #3's
  coskewness (a co-moment, not a tail-state loading) — though all three live on
  the "priced disaster sensitivity" axis and the prereg should report their
  overlap. Honest power caveat: the estimator pools the whole CRSP cross-section
  each month; our ~250-name universe gives a much thinner tail sample, so Hill
  estimates will be noisier — the prereg needs a frozen minimum-names /
  minimum-tail-observations rule, and "estimator too noisy on this universe" is
  an acceptable honest verdict.
- **Dedupe:** PASS — no registry experiment or prior harvest estimates tail
  exponents or tail loadings.

### 4. Gross profitability premium (Novy-Marx)
- **Claim:** Profitability measured as gross profits-to-assets has roughly the
  same power as book-to-market for predicting the cross-section of returns —
  profitable firms earn significantly higher returns than unprofitable firms
  despite higher valuation ratios.
- **Source:** Novy-Marx, "The Other Side of Value: The Gross Profitability
  Premium," *Journal of Financial Economics* 108 (2013), 1–28 (published PDF
  fetched here).
- **Source URL:** https://oldschoolvalue-files.s3.amazonaws.com/pdf/Novy-Marx_Gross-Profitability-Anomaly_JFE_2013.pdf
- **Timeframe:** Annual accounting data; the paper employs a fiscal year's data
  starting at the end of June of the following calendar year (verified in the
  fetched text), monthly/annual hold thereafter. Daily prices, annual formation.
- **Mechanically definable:** Yes — gross profitability = (revenue − cost of
  goods sold) / total assets from completed filings; rank cross-sectionally.
- **PIT data:** Yes, under a discipline that must be frozen at preregistration —
  inputs are revenue, COGS and total assets from 10-K filings, used ONLY after
  the filing's EDGAR filed date, or under the paper's own June-lag convention,
  whichever the prereg adopts. A pull of as-restated-today accounting values
  would fail PIT and is disallowed. This is the first fundamentals claim in the
  harvest where the published design itself carries the lag that makes PIT
  achievable.
- **Strategy family:** `gross_profitability`
- **Why testable:** The harvest's first profitability/quality claim and a
  genuinely different information axis (income-statement productivity, not
  prices). Costs: it needs a public EDGAR XBRL extraction route — free, no
  vendor, but new plumbing for this program. Adjacency flag vs the registry's
  VCP records (`fundamental-momentum-selection`, HOLD): VCP is a price-pattern
  plus quality-screen *selection study* and new VCP claims beyond Phase 1A are
  barred; this is a standalone single-ratio cross-sectional sort, not a VCP
  variant — flagged here for human confirmation at preregistration, not hidden.
- **Dedupe:** PASS with adjacency flag (vs VCP Phase 1A record) — no registry
  experiment sorts on gross profitability.

### 5. Asset growth effect (Cooper–Gulen–Schill)
- **Claim:** A firm's year-on-year percentage change in total assets strongly
  negatively predicts its subsequent abnormal returns — in the summary version,
  low-asset-growth stocks maintained a ~20% per year premium over high-growth
  stocks, persisting up to five years and holding even among large caps.
- **Source:** Cooper, Gulen & Schill, "Asset Growth and the Cross-Section of
  Stock Returns," *Journal of Finance* 63 (2008), 1609–1651 (SSRN version
  fetched here).
- **Source URL:** https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=760967
- **Timeframe:** Annual balance-sheet data (year-on-year total-asset change);
  returns measured from the January following the measurement year in the
  summary version, premium persists up to five years. Daily prices, annual
  formation.
- **Mechanically definable:** Yes — one ratio from two consecutive completed
  balance sheets; rank; no discretion.
- **PIT data:** Yes, under the same frozen discipline as candidate #4 — total
  assets from 10-K filings used only after their EDGAR filed dates (or the
  standard June-lag convention). As-restated pulls are disallowed.
- **Strategy family:** `asset_growth`
- **Why testable:** The investment axis — the other half (with #4) of what later
  became the Fama–French five-factor RMW/CMA pair. Ranked below profitability
  deliberately: the two claims are correlated by construction (expanding firms
  dilute measured profitability), so testing one informs the other and the menu
  should not pretend they are independent draws; the paper itself shows the
  effect survives controlling for accruals, which is also why the accrual
  candidate below is a successor, not a co-listing. Same EDGAR plumbing as #4 —
  if that route is ever built for one, the marginal cost of the other is small.
- **Dedupe:** PASS — no registry experiment or prior harvest sorts on balance-
  sheet growth; not a VCP claim (no price pattern, no quality screen).

### 6. Insider net buying — open-market purchases by officers and directors
- **Claim:** Firms with extensive insider open-market purchases outperform firms
  with extensive insider sales over the following 12 months — by 7.8% raw, 4.8%
  after size and book-to-market controls; the information is in purchases (sales
  carry little predictive content).
- **Source:** Lakonishok & Lee, "Are Insider Trades Informative?" *Review of
  Financial Studies* 14 (2001), 79–111 (published-paper summary fetched here;
  the shortlist entry cites the primary paper per spec).
- **Source URL:** https://www.insidermonkey.com/blog/insider-trading-returns-calculated-by-josef-lakonishok-and-inmoo-lee-546/
- **Timeframe:** Form 4 filings accumulated over a trailing window (paper:
  portfolios formed on insider-trading intensity, returns over the following
  12 months); monthly formation. Daily lane.
- **Mechanically definable:** Yes — net purchase intensity = a frozen function
  (e.g. purchases minus sales, count- or value-based) of open-market Form 4
  transactions in the completed window; rank; no discretion.
- **PIT data:** Yes — and this is the rare non-price source whose PIT timestamp
  is native: SEC Form 4 filings carry filing timestamps (due within two business
  days since 2002) and EDGAR is public and free. Load-bearing rule for the
  prereg: the signal must be keyed to the FILING date, never the transaction
  date — follow-ups that measured returns from the transaction date credited
  outsiders with information they did not yet have.
- **Strategy family:** `insider_net_buying`
- **Why testable:** A disclosed-behaviour signal from a completely different
  data type than anything in the registry or prior menus. Two honest caveats,
  both from the paper itself: the effect is more informative in smaller firms,
  and purchases tilt to small/high-book-to-market names — our 250-name,
  large-cap-tilted universe may dilute it, so a prereg should state the expected
  cross-sectional spread in insider activity on this universe before any return
  gate. Long-only form (overweight intensive net buyers) is the honest version:
  the paper gives the short side little support, and fabricating one would also
  collide with the killed short-side daily family.
- **Dedupe:** PASS — no registry experiment or prior harvest uses regulatory
  filings as a signal.

### 7. Expected idiosyncratic skewness (Boyer–Mitton–Vorkink)
- **Claim:** Stocks with low *expected* idiosyncratic skewness earn high
  returns and vice versa — the low-expected-skewness quintile's Fama–French
  alpha exceeds the high quintile's by 1.00% per month — because skewness-
  preferring ("lotto") investors bid up positively skewed stocks.
- **Source:** Boyer, Mitton & Vorkink, "Expected Idiosyncratic Skewness,"
  *Review of Financial Studies* 23 (2010), 169–202 (implementation summary of
  the primary paper fetched here; the entry cites the primary paper per spec).
- **Source URL:** https://www.quantconnect.com/research/15260/expected-idiosyncratic-skewness/p1
- **Timeframe:** Daily returns within each month give idiosyncratic residuals
  and realized skewness; expected skewness is forecast by a cross-sectional
  model on completed characteristics; monthly formation, one-month hold. Daily
  lane.
- **Mechanically definable:** Yes — residuals from a frozen factor regression,
  sample skewness of residuals, and a frozen cross-sectional forecasting model
  (lagged skewness, lagged idiosyncratic volatility, turnover, momentum, size /
  exchange / industry dummies); no discretion once the model is frozen. Note
  the claim is about the *model-forecast* of skewness, not raw lagged skewness,
  which the paper shows does not forecast well on its own.
- **PIT data:** Yes — daily closes and volume (turnover), all completed before
  formation. One vintage rule for the prereg: factor returns used to form
  residuals must be as available at formation, or residuals must come from a
  market model on the stock's own completed data, which is fully PIT by
  construction.
- **Strategy family:** `expected_idiosyncratic_skewness`
- **Why testable:** Ranked last on purpose — the lottery axis is the most
  crowded corner of the menu (MAX in Harvest #1, IVOL in #2, coskewness in #3),
  and Barinov's follow-up (fetched this run) shows an aggregate-volatility
  factor explains 70–100% of this effect and of MAX in quintile sorts. This
  candidate therefore teaches something new ONLY if it is tested after, or
  jointly with, candidate #2 with that factor controlled; offered now so the
  cluster is complete on the menu and Mike can sequence it knowingly.
- **Dedupe:** PASS with heavy adjacency flags (vs Harvest #1 IH02 MAX, #2 IH01
  IVOL, #3 coskewness) — no registry experiment forecasts idiosyncratic
  skewness; not a re-parameterization of a killed family.
