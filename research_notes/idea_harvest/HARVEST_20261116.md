## Ranked shortlist

### 1. Downside beta — premium for covarying with the market on down days
- **Claim:** Stocks that covary strongly with the market when the market declines earn higher subsequent returns (~6%/yr in the paper) — compensation for downside risk, not for ordinary beta.
- **Source:** Ang, Chen & Xing, "Downside Risk," *Review of Financial Studies* 19 (2006), 1191–1239 (NBER WP 11824).
- **Source URL:** https://ideas.repec.org/p/nbr/nberwo/11824.html
- **Timeframe:** Daily returns; downside beta estimated on down-market days over a trailing window, monthly formation/hold. Daily lane.
- **Mechanically definable:** Yes — downside beta = covariance with the market conditional on market return below its mean, over completed days only; rank cross-sectionally, no discretion.
- **PIT data:** Yes — daily closes for the stock and a market index only; estimate uses only days completed before formation.
- **Strategy family:** `downside_beta`
- **Why testable:** Runs entirely on daily data we already handle, on the frozen universe; monthly turnover is cheap. Long-only form = overweight the high-downside-beta group (the paper's premium is positive for bearing that risk). Frozen gate sketch: forward return of high- vs low-downside-beta groups at 25 bps, with ordinary beta, IVOL (Harvest #2 IH01) and coskewness (Harvest #3 IH05) controlled — a pass that is just beta or IVOL re-labelled teaches nothing. Honest warning carried in the intake: Atilgan et al. (2020, fetched this run) find the premium does not survive value-weighting / fuller controls — that disagreement is exactly what a frozen gate would settle on our universe.
- **Dedupe:** PASS — no registry experiment sorts on downside-conditional beta; distinct from Harvest #4's aggregate-volatility beta (sensitivity to volatility *innovations*) and tail-risk beta (loading on a common tail factor): this is plain market covariance measured only on down days.

### 2. Post-earnings-announcement drift (PEAD) — SUE-sorted drift
- **Claim:** After a quarterly earnings announcement, prices keep drifting in the direction of the earnings surprise for ~60 trading days — the largest positive surprises drift up, the largest negative surprises drift down.
- **Source:** Bernard & Thomas, "Post-Earnings-Announcement Drift: Delayed Price Response or Risk Premium?" *Journal of Accounting Research* 27 Suppl. (1989), 1–36; companion *Journal of Accounting and Economics* 13 (1990), 305–340.
- **Source URL:** https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228431046_The_Post_Earnings_Announcement_Drift_Market_Reactions_to_SEC_Filings_and_the_Information_Environment
- **Timeframe:** Daily/event — SUE formed at each quarterly announcement (seasonal-random-walk surprise: this quarter's EPS vs same quarter last year, scaled), drift window ~60 trading days after the announcement. Daily lane.
- **Mechanically definable:** Yes — SUE deciles from announced EPS only, entry after the announcement, fixed 60-day hold; no discretion once the announcement-date convention is frozen.
- **PIT data:** Yes, with discipline — inputs are the announcement date and the EPS *as announced* (not later restatements), both obtainable from EDGAR filings / announcement records; the prereg must pin the as-announced source and forbid restated EPS. The analyst-consensus version of SUE is NOT claimed PIT here (historical consensus snapshots are the hard version); the seasonal-random-walk version is the testable one.
- **Strategy family:** `post_earnings_announcement_drift`
- **Why testable:** Event-driven, daily lane, ~4 events per name per year on the frozen universe gives a real observation count. Long-only form = buy the top-SUE group for the drift window; the bottom-SUE leg is an *avoid* signal, not a short book (short-side daily is a killed family). FLAGGED adjacent to Harvest #5 IH04 `earnings_announcement_premium`: that claim holds names *around* scheduled announcement dates regardless of what is announced; this claim sorts on the *surprise itself* and holds *after* the announcement — different signal, different window, flagged per spec. Follow-up literature (fetched this run) warns the prolonged drift weakened outside microcaps after the 1990s and is cost-sensitive — the gate must charge real costs and report the liquid-name subset separately.
- **Dedupe:** PASS with adjacency flag (vs Harvest #5 IH04) — no registry experiment sorts on earnings surprise; not a re-parameterization of any killed family.

### 3. S&P 500 index inclusion — announcement-to-effective price pressure, and its modern reversal
- **Claim:** Stocks added to the S&P 500 rose ~3%+ around announcement/inclusion on index-fund demand (price pressure, partly reversing within weeks); follow-up work finds the sign flipped negative for upward additions in 2016–2020 — the live question is which regime holds now.
- **Source:** Harris & Gurel, "Price and Volume Effects Associated with Changes in the S&P 500 List," *Journal of Finance* 41 (1986); Shleifer (1986); survey and modern evidence fetched this run.
- **Source URL:** https://www.thefreelibrary.com/What+drives+the+S%26P+500+inclusion+effect%3F+An+analytical+survey-a0156202951
- **Timeframe:** Daily/event — announcement date (published ~5 trading days before the effective date since Oct 1989) to effective date, plus a 2-week post window for the reversal leg. Daily lane, event only.
- **Mechanically definable:** Yes — announced additions/deletions are public list changes; entry/exit are date rules, no discretion.
- **PIT data:** Yes — S&P announces changes in advance (that advance notice is the tradeable fact); daily bars only.
- **Strategy family:** `index_inclusion_price_pressure`
- **Why testable:** Zero estimation, public event list, daily data we have. Ranked third despite perfect mechanics: only a handful of additions touch our universe per year, so a verdict is slow, and the modern literature (fetched this run: negative announcement returns 2016–2020 for S&P 400→500 moves) says the classic long-the-addition form may now be backwards — a prereg must freeze *both* the classic and flipped hypotheses as separate, pre-committed legs or not run at all. Deletions are treated as avoid-only (no short book).
- **Dedupe:** PASS — no registry or prior-harvest experiment is index-event based; no family match.

### 4. Dividend initiation drift (long-only; omissions as avoid)
- **Claim:** Firms initiating a cash dividend drift up ~+7.5% market-adjusted over the following 12 months; firms omitting a dividend drift down ~−11.0% — a slow drift, distinct from the 3-day announcement reaction (−7.0% omissions / +3.4% initiations).
- **Source:** Michaely, Thaler & Womack, "Price Reactions to Dividend Initiations and Omissions: Overreaction or Drift?" *Journal of Finance* 50 (1995), 573–608.
- **Source URL:** http://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v50y1995i2p573-608.html
- **Timeframe:** Daily/event formation at the initiation announcement; 12-month hold (paper excludes the event month). Weekly/monthly lane fit for the hold.
- **Mechanically definable:** Yes — initiation = first cash dividend after a no-dividend spell, from the announced dividend record; fixed hold, no discretion.
- **PIT data:** Yes — dividend announcements and payment history are public as announced; daily bars only.
- **Strategy family:** `dividend_initiation_drift`
- **Why testable:** Clean event definition on data we can source as-announced. Only the initiation leg is proposed long; omissions are an *avoid/exit* signal, never a short (short-side daily is killed). Ranked fourth: initiations inside a large-cap-tilted 250-name universe are infrequent, so observations accumulate slowly, and the 12-month hold makes any verdict slow — stated plainly in the intake.
- **Dedupe:** PASS — no registry experiment or prior harvest covers dividend-policy events; distinct from Harvest #5's net share issuance (share-count change, not a payout-policy announcement).

### 5. Open-market repurchase announcement drift (value-stock form)
- **Claim:** Firms announcing open-market share repurchases earn ~12.1% abnormal buy-and-hold return over the following four years; for high book-to-market ("value") announcers the figure is ~45.3%, and ~zero for "glamour" announcers.
- **Source:** Ikenberry, Lakonishok & Vermaelen, "Market Underreaction to Open Market Share Repurchases," *Journal of Financial Economics* 39 (1995), 181–208.
- **Source URL:** http://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jfinec/v39y1995i2-3p181-208.html
- **Timeframe:** Daily/event formation at the repurchase announcement; long hold (paper: up to 4 years; a prereg would freeze a shorter 12-month window and say so as a deviation). Monthly lane.
- **Mechanically definable:** Yes — announcement event + the paper's book-to-market split for the value form; no discretion once the window is frozen.
- **PIT data:** Yes — repurchase announcements are public filings/releases as announced; book-to-market uses only already-filed book equity (see candidate 6's lag caveat).
- **Strategy family:** `repurchase_announcement_drift`
- **Why testable:** A management-signal event, not a price pattern. FLAGGED adjacent on two sides: Harvest #5 IH02 `net_share_issuance` (that sorts on *actual* share-count change over a year; this is the *announcement* event, and many announced programs are never completed — the prereg must not substitute completed-buyback data, which would be look-ahead) and Harvest #4 IH06 `insider_net_buying` (a repurchase is the firm buying, not officers buying personally — different actor, different filing). Ranked fifth for the very long original horizon and the completion/look-ahead trap.
- **Dedupe:** PASS with adjacency flags — no registry experiment covers repurchase announcements.

### 6. Book-to-market value premium (long-only high-B/M tilt)
- **Claim:** High book-to-market (value) stocks earn higher average returns than low book-to-market (growth) stocks — the HML premium.
- **Source:** Fama & French, "Common Risk Factors in the Returns on Stocks and Bonds," *Journal of Financial Economics* 33 (1993), 3–56; cross-section in Fama & French, *Journal of Finance* 47 (1992).
- **Source URL:** https://www.convexpi.ai/papers/72412e20-d407-442a-b090-d9a334d68cab
- **Timeframe:** Monthly/annual — B/M formed once a year from filed book equity and market cap, held 12 months. Weekly/monthly lane.
- **Mechanically definable:** Yes — B/M = book equity / market equity, rank, hold the high-B/M group; no discretion once the filing-lag rule is frozen.
- **PIT data:** Partial — prices are PIT, but book equity is only PIT if the prereg enforces a conservative lag (e.g. only fiscal years filed ≥6 months before formation, the Fama-French convention). Our fundamentals route is exactly the one that has been hard in this program (the VCP Phase 1A EDGAR work), so this is demoted to partial despite the classic lag fix existing on paper.
- **Strategy family:** `book_to_market_value`
- **Why testable:** The most replicated characteristic in finance, never yet intake-tested here as a standalone claim. Long-only high-B/M tilt judged at portfolio level. Demoted to sixth on PIT-data grounds only (testability rubric: partial data demotes), and FLAGGED: it is the conditioning variable inside candidate 5's value form and a control in several earlier intakes — a standalone test must not double-count those.
- **Dedupe:** PASS — no registry experiment sorts on book-to-market as the primary claim (VCP used fundamental *quality/momentum selection*, a different mechanism; B/M appeared there only as a control).

### 7. Customer momentum — economic links (supplier follows its major customer)
- **Claim:** A supplier's stock follows its principal customer's stock with a lag: sorting suppliers on their customers' prior-month return earned >150 bps/month long-short alpha in the paper, because investors ignore the disclosed customer link.
- **Source:** Cohen & Frazzini, "Economic Links and Predictable Returns," *Journal of Finance* 63 (2008), 1977–2011.
- **Source URL:** https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~afrazzin/pdf/Economic%20Links%20and%20Predictable%20Returns%20-%20Cohen%20and%20Frazzini.pdf
- **Timeframe:** Monthly — customer return in month t predicts supplier return in month t+1. Monthly lane.
- **Mechanically definable:** Yes, *given* the link map — customer return is a number; the discretion risk sits entirely in building the link map, which the prereg must freeze as a dated snapshot.
- **PIT data:** Unknown / partial — links come from segment disclosures (customers >10% of sales, SFAS 131) in 10-Ks; historical link maps as *first disclosed* (not as later restated/rebuilt by a vendor today) are the PIT problem, and Compustat segment history is a vendor product we do not hold. Demoted for exactly this.
- **Strategy family:** `customer_momentum_economic_links`
- **Why testable:** Genuinely different mechanism (cross-firm information diffusion along a disclosed supply chain). FLAGGED adjacent to Harvest #4 IH01 `industry_lead_lag_diffusion` (big firms lead small firms *within an industry*): this uses *named firm-to-firm customer links*, which cross industries — different link definition, flagged per spec. Long-only form = overweight suppliers whose customers just rose; the short leg is not proposed.
- **Dedupe:** PASS with adjacency flag — no registry experiment uses firm-to-firm customer links.

### 8. Search attention (abnormal Google search volume, ASVI)
- **Claim:** A spike in Google search volume for a stock's ticker predicts price pressure over the next 1–2 weeks (retail attention buying), with reversal over the following year; the effect is strongest in smaller, retail-held names and around IPOs.
- **Source:** Da, Engelberg & Gao, "In Search of Attention," *Journal of Finance* 66 (2011), 1461–1499.
- **Source URL:** https://phys.org/news/2011-02-stock-google.html
- **Timeframe:** Weekly — ASVI = this week's search volume vs its trailing median, 1–2 week forward window (weekly lane).
- **Mechanically definable:** Yes — ASVI is an exact formula on the search series, no discretion.
- **PIT data:** No / unknown — Google Trends search-volume index values are renormalized and resampled when re-pulled, so a series downloaded today is not the series that was live at the decision date; there is no as-published archive we can access. That fails the PIT leg of the rubric and is why this ranks last despite a clean rule and a perfect weekly-lane fit. It becomes testable only prospectively (capture the series forward, first-seen, from now on).
- **Strategy family:** `search_attention_asvi`
- **Why testable:** Included deliberately as the honest tail of the list: a real, replicated claim whose *only* blocker is point-in-time data integrity — the same wall as Harvest #1's intraday candidates. A prospective first-seen capture is the sole honest route; any backtest on re-pulled Trends data would be testing revised numbers.
- **Dedupe:** PASS — no registry experiment or prior harvest uses search/attention data; the volume-based intakes (Harvest #1 volume premium, Harvest #2 Amihud) use *trading* volume, not search volume.
