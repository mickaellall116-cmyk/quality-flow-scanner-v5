## Ranked shortlist

### 1. Overnight vs intraday return decomposition ("tug of war")
- **Claim:** A stock's recent overnight returns (close→open) and intraday returns
  (open→close) carry opposite, persistent premia — high recent overnight returners
  keep earning overnight but give it back intraday, so each leg is separately predictable.
- **Source:** Lou, Polk & Skouras, "A Tug of War: Overnight versus Intraday Expected
  Returns," *Journal of Financial Economics* 134 (2019), 192–213.
- **Source URL:** https://researchonline.lse.ac.uk/id/eprint/87481/ (open-access version;
  also indexed at https://EconPapers.repec.org/paper/ehllserod/87481.htm)
- **Timeframe:** Daily bars; trailing ~1-month component formation, monthly hold.
- **Mechanically definable:** Yes — overnight leg = open/prev close − 1, intraday leg =
  close/open − 1, rank cross-sectionally, no discretion.
- **PIT data:** Yes — daily open/high/low/close only; open prices are recorded at the
  open, component ranks use only completed days.
- **Strategy family:** `overnight_intraday_decomposition`
- **Why testable:** Runs entirely on daily OHLC we already handle; fits the daily lane
  on the frozen 250-symbol universe; a frozen gate would compare each leg's forward
  return vs the close-to-close baseline at 25 bps, long-only legs reported separately
  (short-side daily is a known structural loser here, so the intraday-reversal leg
  would be tested as an *avoid/underweight* signal, not a short book).
- **Dedupe:** PASS — no registry experiment tests return-component decomposition;
  no `attempted_variations` or `failure_strategy_family` match.

### 2. MAX effect — avoid lottery-like stocks (maximum daily return)
- **Claim:** Stocks with the highest single-day return over the past month
  underperform over the following month — investors overpay for lottery-like payoffs.
- **Source:** Bali, Cakici & Whitelaw, "Maxing Out: Stocks as Lotteries and the
  Cross-Section of Expected Returns," *Journal of Financial Economics* 99 (2011), 427–446.
- **Source URL:** https://paperswithbacktest.com/api/paper/maxing-out-stocks-as-lotteries-and-the-cross-section-of-expected/pdf
  (working paper: SSRN abstract 1262416)
- **Timeframe:** Daily returns; past-1-month MAX formation, 1-month hold, weekly lane fit.
- **Mechanically definable:** Yes — MAX = max daily return in trailing month (paper also
  uses the average of the 5 largest); rank and exclude/underweight the top group.
- **PIT data:** Yes — daily closes only.
- **Strategy family:** `lottery_max`
- **Why testable:** Long-only implementation matches how we trade: a MAX *exclusion
  filter* or underweight on names we would otherwise buy, judged at portfolio level
  inside the stack (per the standing rule: filters are judged in the portfolio, never
  on isolated expectancy). Needs a broad cross-section — the 250-symbol universe, not
  the 14-name watchlist.
- **Dedupe:** PASS — no registry entry covers lottery/skewness characteristics;
  distinct mechanism from the killed TOP/composite filters (those ranked on
  return/quality composites, not on an extreme-return characteristic).

### 3. High-volume return premium
- **Claim:** Stocks with unusually high trading volume over a day or week tend to
  appreciate over the following month; unusually low volume precedes depreciation
  (visibility / investor-recognition mechanism).
- **Source:** Gervais, Kaniel & Mingelgrin, "The High-Volume Return Premium,"
  *Journal of Finance* 56 (2001), 877–919.
- **Source URL:** https://sites.duke.edu/sgervais/research/gervais-kaniel-mingelgrin-2001/
  (author's page with reference and abstract)
- **Timeframe:** Daily OHLCV; abnormal-volume formation over a day/week vs a trailing
  reference window, ~1-month forward window. Daily/weekly lanes.
- **Mechanically definable:** Yes — abnormal volume = formation-window volume vs
  trailing average volume, thresholded; no discretion.
- **PIT data:** Yes, with a caveat — daily volume is in our bars, but vendor volume
  conventions (split adjustment, odd-lot inclusion) must be pinned in the prereg;
  the signal uses only completed days.
- **Strategy family:** `volume_premium`
- **Why testable:** Volume is the one input our current systems barely use; the claim
  is a level/attention effect, not a price-pattern effect, so it is genuinely new
  information for the stack. Frozen gate: forward return of the high-abnormal-volume
  group vs universe baseline at 25 bps, judged inside the portfolio per standing rules.
- **Dedupe:** PASS — no registry experiment uses a volume-formation signal;
  no family match (`orb` used opening-range price, not abnormal volume).

### 4. Nearness to the 52-week high (anchoring momentum)
- **Claim:** The closer a stock's price is to its 52-week high, the better its
  subsequent returns — investors anchor on the high and underreact to good news near it.
- **Source:** George & Hwang, "The 52-Week High and Momentum Investing,"
  *Journal of Finance* 59 (2004), 2145–2176.
- **Source URL:** https://blankcapitalresearch.com/learn/george-hwang-52-week-high
  (research summary of the primary paper; implementation notes also at
  https://github.com/dasanish/personaltrading/blob/HEAD/research/ideas/52-week-high-momentum.md)
- **Timeframe:** Daily closes; nearness = close / trailing-52-week high, monthly
  formation/hold. Daily/weekly lanes.
- **Mechanically definable:** Yes — a single ratio of price to a rolling maximum.
- **PIT data:** Yes — daily highs/closes only.
- **Strategy family:** `anchoring_momentum`
- **Why testable:** Cheapest possible test on data we already have. FLAGGED
  family-adjacent: it is a momentum claim, and momentum/trend is our most-tested
  family (V5.4 `trend_4h` standing FAIL). The mechanism and statistic are genuinely
  different (price-to-rolling-max ratio vs trailing return), and the paper reports
  it subsumes return momentum — but novelty is discounted in the ranking for
  exactly this adjacency.
- **Dedupe:** PASS with adjacency flag — no registry experiment tests
  nearness-to-high; not a re-parameterization of V5.4 (different signal statistic,
  different timeframe, cross-sectional rather than time-series).

### 5. Market intraday momentum (first half-hour → last half-hour)
- **Claim:** The market index's first half-hour return predicts its last half-hour
  return the same day — late-day rebalancing and hedging flows repeat the morning's direction.
- **Source:** Gao, Han, Li & Zhou, "Market Intraday Momentum," *Journal of Financial
  Economics* (2018); working paper SSRN abstract 2552752.
- **Source URL:** https://paperswithbacktest.com/api/paper/intraday-momentum-the-first-half-hour-return-predicts-the-last-half-hour-return/pdf
- **Timeframe:** Intraday — 30-minute windows (09:30–10:00 signal, final half-hour
  holding window), one trade per day, flat overnight.
- **Mechanically definable:** Yes — sign/magnitude of the first half-hour return,
  enter at the start of the last half-hour, exit at the close.
- **PIT data:** **Partial / unknown** — the rule needs 30-minute bars. Our qualified
  history is 4H built from 1H bars; long 30-minute history is exactly the intraday
  data wall this project keeps hitting. Short samples exist on free feeds but are
  too short for a trustworthy verdict today.
- **Strategy family:** `intraday_momentum`
- **Why testable:** One trade a day is the most cost-survivable intraday shape there
  is, and it fits the intraday lane conceptually — but it is ranked fifth, not first,
  because the data prerequisite is unmet. Honest sequencing: this becomes testable
  the day a qualified intraday route exists; testing it on a few weeks of free
  30-minute data would produce a number we could not trust.
- **Dedupe:** PASS — distinct from ORB (`EXP-20261007-ORB-15M`, opening-range
  breakout vs random null): different signal (whole-market first-half-hour return),
  different holding window (last half-hour only), different claim (return
  continuation, not range-break timing).

### 6. Overnight volatility predicts next-day intraday volatility (asymmetric)
- **Claim:** Overnight volatility (absolute overnight log-return) strongly predicts
  the *following* day's intraday volatility, but intraday volatility barely predicts
  the next overnight volatility — a one-way, exploitable volatility forecast.
- **Source:** Zadourian & Grassberger, "Asymmetry of Cross Correlations Between
  Intraday and Overnight Volatilities," arXiv:1509.08079 (2015).
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/1509.08079
- **Timeframe:** Daily — overnight move known at the open forecasts that day's
  intraday range/volatility.
- **Mechanically definable:** Yes — correlation/forecast rule on absolute returns,
  fully specified.
- **PIT data:** Partial — needs intraday bars to measure realized intraday
  volatility at length; the overnight leg alone is available in daily bars, but the
  target variable is not. Same intraday data wall as candidate 5.
- **Strategy family:** `volatility_forecast`
- **Why testable:** This is not a directional edge; it would feed position sizing
  and stop placement (volatility-targeted risk), which our stack currently sets
  statically. Ranked last: useful input, no standalone trade, and data-blocked.
- **Dedupe:** PASS — no registry experiment covers volatility forecasting or
  sizing overlays.
