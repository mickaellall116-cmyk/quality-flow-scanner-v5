## Reel 1 — the Gartley 222 (an entry method)
Claim: Paul's favourite entry/exit methodology is H.M. Gartley's, from *Profits in the Stock Market* (1935), p.222 — the pattern since called the Gartley 222: **a thrust up, then a three-wave pullback**, then entry. Paul notes it is very similar to Wyckoff's upthrust. (The later harmonic-trading literature — Pesavento et al. — overlays Fibonacci ratio requirements on the Gartley pattern; Paul's version as presented is the plain thrust + three-wave pullback, WITHOUT the ratio overlay. Any test must say which version it means; this note means Paul's plain version.)

### What a mechanical version would have to freeze (4H, our house timeframe)
1. THRUST: an impulse leg. Candidate operationalizations: (a) zigzag swing of >= K x ATR(14) with no intervening counter-swing > threshold; (b) close at an N-bar high with range expansion vs trailing median range. Either is a free parameter and a known overfitting surface.
2. THREE-WAVE PULLBACK: an ABC zigzag retracement of the thrust: wave A down, wave B up (must not exceed the thrust high), wave C down (must not break the thrust origin; depth band vs the thrust, e.g. 38-79%, is another free parameter).
3. ENTRY TRIGGER candidates: first 4H close back above the wave-B high; or first close above the final pullback swing high. Stop: below the wave-C low (structural, consistent with house style). Exits, if a test is ever preregistered: the frozen Mode B stack, unchanged — the idea is an ENTRY claim only.
4. PIT/access: computable from bars available at the decision bar (zigzag legs must be confirmed only by bars already printed — the classic repainting trap: a swing is only known after a confirming counter-move; the definition must charge that lag honestly).

### The real hypothesis (incremental value)
Thrust + pullback entries already exist in this shop's world (pullback/flag shapes, the frozen system's own entry family). The falsifiable question is NOT "do Gartley-222 entries make money" but "**does requiring exactly three pullback waves add anything over the generic thrust+pullback entry on the same universe/timeframe?**" A test without that control arm proves nothing about the pattern.

### Known risks
- Wave-counting is threshold-sensitive; small definition changes swing counts and results (the Daily Sweep / inside-bar lessons apply: freeze ONE definition before seeing outcomes).
- Sample size: on a 51-symbol 4H universe the strict version may be rare; the loose version may be indistinguishable from generic pullbacks.
- This is a 1935 pattern described in prose; there is no canonical parameter set to "just use" — every choice is ours and must be frozen pre-data.

## Reel 2 — Wyckoff's three stages (a framing, not a method)
Claim: markets cycle Accumulation -> Markup -> Distribution; the early buyers at the bottom are the sellers at the top; and **distribution feels identical to markup from the inside** (price rising, news good, position green) — the only change is the counterparty, which is invisible by design. Paul "reads the handoff."
Assessment: as stated this is NOT mechanically testable — phase labels are reliable in hindsight and contested in real time; no frozen rule is offered or obvious. Mechanical proxies (long flat base + volume signature -> "accumulation") collapse into a different, already-adjacent hypothesis (base-breakout / VCP-style contraction, which already has its own quarantined lane: the VCP probe). The reel's durable content for this team is the psychology point, which is also the standing argument for frozen rules + forward testing: the stage cannot be felt, so it must not be traded on feel.
