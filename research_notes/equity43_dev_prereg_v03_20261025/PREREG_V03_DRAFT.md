## 1. Purpose and status

This protocol tests three questions about the frozen Quality Flow rule set:

- **H-BUY:** does the BUY signal predict forward returns beyond a matched baseline?
- **H-EXIT:** do the EXIT rules beat a small set of alternatives on identical entries?
- **H-SYS:** is the complete strategy profitable net of conservative costs?

The claim "Quality Flow has an edge" requires H-SYS to PASS on untouched evidence.

**Scope is the frozen rule set.** We test the rules recorded in experiment QF-EQUITY43-DEV-ORDER-CENSOR-20261023-13 (config pin `87b8285a…8706`, source commit `555ebb1`) as they are. Any modified rule is a new version and needs fresh untouched evidence; it cannot reuse the holdout.

Three limits, stated up front:

- **Not blind.** The team has seen DEV results, and Claude saw the DEV aggregates in the blind-review packet. Parameters below were set by reasoning about mechanics, not by tuning to DEV outcomes, but their independence from DEV is unproven and Muse should challenge it.
- **Power is limited.** The ledger shows ~1.8R SD per trade. Testing μ > 0 for a +0.17R expectancy needs ≈693 independent trades; **confirming under this protocol's own PASS rule (lower bound L ≥ δ = 0.05R) needs ≈1,392 independent trades** at the same planning values (one-sided 5%, 80% power, before any design effect) `[C02]`. One year of the 43-name universe will not reach that. INCONCLUSIVE is therefore an expected and acceptable outcome, and thresholds may not be loosened after any result is seen (§6).
- **Scope is the frozen rule set** (above).

This is a draft for Muse's independent review; no new performance test is authorized until the protocol is frozen (§15).

## 4. Hypotheses

Each hypothesis has one estimand, one tier of evidence and one verdict. The null for each H is no effect or a negative effect. Direction is pre-declared and one-sided, because only improvement matters for a go decision. H-BUY, H-EXIT and H-SYS are separate families (§6), and the edge claim rests on H-SYS-1 alone.

| ID | Alternative hypothesis | Estimand | Evidence | Role |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| H-BUY-P | The frozen BUY signal beats its matched baseline at 10 bars | Mean paired excess of ATR-normalized forward return, signal minus baseline (§5) | Tiers 2 to 4 | Primary, gating |
| H-BUY-S5, S20 | Same, at 5 and 20 bars | Same | Tiers 2 to 4 | Secondary, reported only if H-BUY-P passes |
| H-EXIT-1 | An intrabar stop improves on the close-evaluated stop | Mean paired per-trade net R, E1 minus E0, on fixed entries (study 7A) | Tiers 2 to 4 | Gating for the exit verdict |
| H-EXIT-3 | The current exit logic improves on a fixed 20-bar exit | Mean paired per-trade net R, E0 minus E3, on fixed entries (study 7A) | Tiers 2 to 4 | Gating: do the exits add value |
| H-EXIT-4 | An intrabar stop improves the complete strategy when later entries are allowed to change | Difference in mean net R per closed trade, replay with E1 minus replay with E0 (study 7C) | Tiers 2 to 4 | Gating for any exit change to the deployed system |
| R-EXIT-2 | Robustness, not a hypothesis: how much of E0's result depends on touch-equals-fill at TP1 | Mean paired net R, E2 minus E0 (expected ≤ 0) | Tiers 2 to 4 | Reported with a flag; no PASS or FAIL |
| H-SYS-1 | The complete frozen strategy has positive net expectancy at the gating cost | Mean net R per closed trade at 25 bps round trip | Tiers 2 to 4 | Primary for the edge claim |

R-EXIT-2 is outside the Holm family because it is a sensitivity check, not a claim. **It is the only name for the E2 leg anywhere in this protocol**; the v0.2 §13 register row that still read "H-EXIT-2" is renamed in §13 `[C12]`.

**Analysis-boundary censoring (applies to H-SYS-1 and H-EXIT-4) `[C09]`.** The estimands above are means over **closed** trades. A position still open at the analysis boundary is **excluded from the closed-trade mean** — no R is fabricated for it — and is disclosed beside every verdict: the **open count** and its **marked equity** (marked at the boundary close on the all-bar MTM basis, P1). Portfolio-level quantities are a different statement and are never substituted for the closed-trade mean: portfolio return and drawdown are computed on the marked equity path, in which open positions are carried at mark (the -13 precedent: the DEV run's 4 censored taken positions are valued in equity, excluded from closed-trade R). On the DEV record the two statements differ materially — closed-trade mean +0.17373R (4 bps) vs portfolio total return +42.44% with all-bar drawdown 26.1840% — which is why both are always reported and never interchanged. In study 7C each replay reports its own open count and marked equity; the two replays' trade lists, including their open sets, may differ (§7).

## 5. BUY signal study

**Events.** A signal event is any completed 4H session-aligned bar where the frozen pine_buy_signal (V3.6 hybrid: confirmedBuy OR breakoutBuy OR readyBuy) is true after the 215-bar warmup, regardless of portfolio state or any simulated open trade. Primary analysis keeps an event only if the same symbol had no signal bar in the previous 20 bars, so 20-bar windows of one symbol never overlap. Events whose forward window runs past the last bar are dropped, never completed with a marked or invented price. Dropped counts are reported.

**Forward return.** Entry price is the open of the next bar (bar i+1). Exit price is the close of bar i+h. The value is (exit − entry) divided by the signal bar's ATR(14) in price units, which uses only information known at signal time. Percent return is reported alongside.

**Primary matched baseline (B-A).** Same-symbol pool: same time of day (09:30 or 13:30); within 30 trading sessions; no signal bar within 20 bars on either side; ATR% within ±25%; full forward window inside data. Draw 20 pool bars at random without replacement (fixed seed). Require at least 8 eligible bars, or drop the event and report the count; more than 15% dropped is a flag.

**Feasibility check, outcome-blind.** Before any tier is opened, the matching code is run on DEV in a mode that reports only pool sizes: for every event, the count of eligible bars under rules 1 to 5, with no forward returns computed or shown. If more than 15% of events have fewer than 8 eligible bars, the restrictions are relaxed in a fixed, pre-declared order until the drop rate is under 15%: (a) widen the ATR% band to ±40%; (b) widen the session window to ±45 sessions; (c) lower the minimum to 6 eligible bars. The relaxation step reached is recorded and frozen for every tier. No forward return or group difference is examined during this check, so pool-count feasibility cannot leak outcome information into the design. **The feasibility report additionally publishes, still outcome-blind, the dropped-event count and an event-mix composition summary (by symbol and session bin) at the frozen relaxation step** `[C14]`. Pool eligibility, pool counts and mix feasibility are audited under this check only; matching-failure handling is the ladder above and nothing else (see also §15 and `OUTCOME_BLIND_FEASIBILITY.md`).

**Diagnostic baseline (B-T, non-gating).** The same construction, but the pool is limited to bars where close is above EMA200, EMA21 is above EMA55, and the full signal is false. This isolates what the trigger adds beyond a bullish trend state.

**Horizons.** The fixed set is 5, 10 and 20 bars, and **10 bars is the single primary endpoint**; this designation is preserved until freeze. 10 bars is about 5 sessions: long enough to reflect a trend hold, short enough to limit window overlap. Disclosure: this choice was made knowing the DEV average hold of about 17 bars, so it is not fully independent of DEV. Muse may argue for 20 before freeze. The 5- and 20-bar results are interpreted inferentially only if the 10-bar test passes (fixed-sequence); otherwise they are descriptive.

## 6. Statistics

**Inference.** Every verdict compares a dependence-aware one-sided 95% confidence bound with a minimum effect δ.

- **Predeclared interval method (reconciled) `[C10]`.** The primary interval is the **circular block bootstrap over calendar time with blocks of 20 sessions**, 10,000 replications, seed fixed before any data is opened. This adopts the already-frozen inference contract (validation preregistration finalization, commit `fc17041`: circular block bootstrap, 20-session blocks, 10,000 deterministic replications, seed = first 8 bytes, big-endian, of the frozen preregistration's SHA-256) and supersedes v0.2's "moving-block bootstrap, blocks of 20 bars (about 10 sessions)". The deviation in v0.2 was twofold — block unit (bars vs sessions) and moving vs circular; both are resolved here in favour of the frozen contract, and this reconciliation is recorded in the change register so the freeze record shows it was deliberate.
- Robustness interval: a symbol-cluster bootstrap. **The verdict uses the wider of the two intervals.**
- In study 7C the block bootstrap is applied to both replays jointly (same blocks, both arms), not as a per-trade pairing.
- Always report n, the number of blocks, the design effect (cluster variance divided by independent-observation variance) and n_eff = n / design effect.

**Thresholds.** PASS requires the lower bound L ≥ δ.

| Test | PASS requires | δ (minimum effect) | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|
| H-BUY-P | Lower bound L ≥ δ | 0.10 ATR | About 25 bps at a ~2.6% ATR (from the DEV median stop distance of 3.84% = 1.5 ATR; a descriptive figure, not a performance one) |
| H-EXIT-1, -3, -4 | Holm-adjusted lower bound L ≥ δ | 0.10 R per trade | Improvement must exceed a conservative round-trip cost |
| H-SYS-1 | Lower bound L ≥ δ | 0.05 R per trade | About 5 bps of equity per trade at 1% risk, roughly 5% a year at the DEV trade frequency |

α is one-sided 0.05 throughout; the H-EXIT family is familywise one-sided 0.05 with Holm (below); H-SYS is a single test at one-sided 0.05. Minimum effects and gating cost: 0.10 ATR, 0.10 R, 0.05 R, 25 bps round trip.

**Reported labels.** CONFIRMED AT δ (L ≥ δ); POSITIVE, NOT CONFIRMED AT δ (0 < L < δ); INDETERMINATE (L ≤ 0 and U ≥ δ); EXCLUDED (U < δ). Only the first maps to PASS and only the last to FAIL. PASS means the interval shows, with confidence, an effect of at least δ; it is not enough that the effect is positive and the point estimate looks large.

**Validity floors.** Below these the verdict is INCONCLUSIVE regardless of the estimate: 200 de-clustered events for H-BUY, 100 closed trades for H-EXIT and H-SYS, and at least 30 independent blocks and 20 symbols in every test.

**Power report, required before any tier is opened.** Estimate the outcome SD and design effect from variance only: pool forward returns and baseline returns without computing any group difference, and have an analyst report just the SD and design effect. From these, publish the expected interval half-width for each tier, and — per endpoint — the **dependence-aware strict-δ power at that endpoint's own δ, its α after family allocation (Holm worst case for H-EXIT), the paired SD for the 7A paired endpoints, and the design effect** `[C03]`.

Planning constants, carried from v0.2 as its stated planning figures and **not re-estimated**: per-trade SD σ = 1.8R (from the ledger); paired 10-bar excess SD σ = 2.6 ATR (Claude's estimate); planning expectancy μ = +0.17R for H-SYS-1. With z₀.₉₅ = 1.6449 and z₀.₈₀ = 0.8416 (sum 2.4865), three different sample sizes answer three different questions and must never be conflated:

| Question | Formula basis | H-SYS-1 (δ = 0.05R, σ = 1.8R) | H-BUY-P (δ = 0.10 ATR, σ = 2.6 ATR) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Test μ > 0 at μ = +0.17R, 80% power | ((2.4865 · σ) / μ)² | **≈693** independent trades | — (no planning μ declared) |
| Strict confirmation L ≥ δ at μ = +0.17R, 80% power | ((2.4865 · σ) / (μ − δ))² | **≈1,392** independent trades | — (no planning μ declared) |
| Interval half-width = δ (≈50% power for strict PASS at true effect 2δ) | ((1.6449 · σ) / δ)² | **≈3,506** independent trades | **≈1,829** independent events |
| Strict confirmation L ≥ δ at true effect **2δ**, 80% power | ((2.4865 · σ) / δ)² | **≈8,013** independent trades | **≈4,179** independent events |

All figures are independent-observation approximations, **before** the design effect; effective requirements rise by the design effect.

**Correction (N1) `[C04]`.** v0.2 stated: "Reaching L ≥ δ for a true effect of 2δ needs about the same [as bringing the half-width down to δ]." That sentence is **replaced**. The equality holds only at ≈50% power, where the requirement equals the half-width figure (≈3,506) by construction. At this protocol's 80%-power convention, a true effect of 2δ needs **≈8,013 independent trades (H-SYS-1) / ≈4,179 independent events (H-BUY-P)** — about 2.3× the half-width figure. A test whose expected half-width exceeds δ is labelled UNDERPOWERED FOR PASS: a PASS is unlikely even if the effect is real. It can still FAIL, because a sufficiently negative result (U < δ) is possible at any sample size, and it can still be INCONCLUSIVE. The label is reported with the verdict. The team decides before opening, not after, whether to add Tier 3 symbols or wait for more forward data.

**Multiple testing.** H-BUY: fixed sequence (10 bars first, then 5 and 20), so no alpha is spent on the secondaries. H-EXIT: **Holm correction across H-EXIT-1, H-EXIT-3 and H-EXIT-4** at a familywise one-sided 0.05; ties in the step-down ordering are broken by the fixed hypothesis order (1, 3, 4) so the procedure is deterministic `[C23]`. **R-EXIT-2 is a reported sensitivity and takes no alpha.** H-SYS: a single test at one-sided 0.05. The three families answer different questions and are not pooled, and the edge claim rests on H-SYS-1 alone. Any hypothesis generated from a DEV look is registered as new and tested only on untouched evidence, with Holm correction across all hypotheses generated from the same DEV exploration.

## 7. EXIT study

Exits are tested three ways, because a paired comparison on fixed entries and a replay of the whole strategy answer different questions. The arm set is frozen now: no arm may be added or altered after any DEV result is seen, and a new exit idea is a new rule version that needs fresh evidence.

**Fidelity to the frozen strategy.** Every arm starts from the frozen simulator's own terms: entry at the next bar's open, initial stop = signal-bar close − 1.5 × ATR(14), TP1 = fill + 2.0 × ATR(14), both from the signal bar (gen_candidates_censored, matching pine_backtest.py lines 150 and 157). Note that the simulator fixes TP1 from signal-bar ATR while the original Pine script recomputes it live; the tests match the rules as frozen and run, not TradingView. Precondition P9 (byte-identical reproduction of the -13 ledger, staged per §3/§8) is the proof that E0 is the strategy and not a look-alike.

**Study 7A, paired on event-study entries.** Entries are the de-clustered signal events from section 5, run as standalone trades with no ranking, sector cap or position limit. Events that gap below the initial stop are excluded from all arms. This isolates the exit rule on the cleanest, largest entry set, but the entries are not the ones the deployed strategy would take.

> **Scope of the 7A exclusion (corrected wording; N2 as errata'd at `f625921`) `[C11]`.** The exclusion concerns events whose **next-bar entry open is already below the signal-bar stop threshold** — entries that would open already stopped, i.e. never-valid entries. It is a **pre-entry** gap exclusion; it is **not** an exclusion of gaps through a held position's stop (a held position's gap behaviour is part of what the exit arms themselves govern). Accordingly: **a 7A verdict generalises only to entries that open at or above the initial stop threshold; 7A is silent on the never-valid-entry subpopulation.** Study 7C retains all trades, so the system-level question is unaffected.

**Study 7B, paired on the strategy's own candidates (non-gating, selection-conditioned).** The same arms on the 586-style closed candidate set that the frozen strategy generates. Caution: that candidate set is itself produced by E0, because each standalone trade suppresses later same-symbol signals until E0 exits. Comparing other exits on entries that E0 chose is a selection effect in E0's favor, so 7B is descriptive and carries no verdict.

**Study 7C, full-strategy replay (separate; requires its own future GO).** The complete frozen pipeline (signal, suppression, ranking, sector cap, position limit, sizing) is re-run with E1 in place of E0, so a different exit changes which later candidates exist and are taken. The estimand is the difference in mean net R per closed trade between the two replays. **The two trade lists differ by design**; inference uses the block bootstrap over calendar time applied to both replays jointly (same blocks, both arms), not a per-trade pairing. **Beside the mean-R estimand, 7C reports for both replays: trade counts, exposure, drawdown, the open-at-boundary count and marked equity (§4), and the equity/time-normalised portfolio return of each replay — the portfolio return is reported alongside the mean R and the two are never substituted for each other** `[C08]`. H-EXIT-4 is the test; it is the only exit result that speaks to the system as deployed.

**Arms.**

| Arm | Stop | TP1 | Other exits | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| E0 (control) | Close below the stop level, exit at next open; runner stop = max(stop, close − 2.5 ATR) after TP1 | 50% at the limit, filled when the bar's high touches it | Close below EMA55 or trend-bear, next open | The current rules |
| E1 | Intrabar: fill at min(open, stop) with 0.05 ATR extra slippage; stop-first if stop and TP1 are both touched in one bar | Same as E0 | Same as E0 | Cost and benefit of exiting before the candle closes |
| E2 | Same as E0 | Fills only if the high exceeds TP1 by 0.05 ATR | Same as E0 | Robustness only (R-EXIT-2): how much E0 depends on touch-equals-fill optimism; expected to lower E0, not improve it |
| E3 (reference) | None | None | Fixed exit at the close of bar i+20 | Do the exits add anything beyond holding the signal |

**Declared modelling assumptions `[C15]`.** The two **0.05 ATR** constants in this table — E1's extra stop slippage and E2's TP1 exceed-margin — carry no empirical provenance in the record. They are **declared modelling assumptions**, adopted as conventions of the study design, and are labelled as such wherever they are used. (This labelling does not apply to δ = 0.05R for H-SYS-1, which is a minimum effect with its own stated rationale in §6.)

**Metric.** The gating metric in 7A is the paired per-trade net R difference at the gating cost, written so that a positive value favors the alternative: E1 minus E0 for H-EXIT-1, and E0 minus E3 for H-EXIT-3. R-EXIT-2 reports E2 minus E0 and is expected to be at or below zero; a value below −0.10R raises a FILL-OPTIMISM flag on every E0-based result.

**Oversized losses (non-gating, always reported).** Where persisted bars allow it, per-trade loss decomposition (planned −1R, excursion to trigger close, gap from that close to the next open) is reported for every arm; fields that were never persisted are reported as NOT_RECOVERABLE, never backfilled.

**TP1 assumptions.** The base rule fills TP1 when the bar's high touches the limit. A touched high does not by itself prove an executable fill (queue/liquidity); E2/R-EXIT-2 is the standing sensitivity for that optimism.

**Evidence.** Any exit change to the deployed system requires H-EXIT-4 to PASS; a 7A PASS alone does not justify changing the strategy.

## 8. Costs and execution assumptions

All verdicts use a conservative 25 bps round-trip cost applied per fill, because no real fill record exists for any trade in the record. The 4 and 10 bps results are reported as sensitivities and never decide a verdict.

**Per-fill model, stated as an accounting convention.** Cost for each fill = 12.5 bps × (shares filled × fill price), charged in dollars at the fill. Trade net P&L in dollars = Σ(exit fills) − entry fill − Σ(fill costs); net R = that figure divided by risk dollars (shares × (entry − initial stop)). Because exit notional moves with price, total cost is not exactly 25 bps of entry notional: a trade exited 10% higher pays about 26.25 bps of entry notional, one stopped 4% lower about 24.5 bps. This is intended; it is how a broker charges. The 25 bps figure is the round-trip cost at unchanged price and is the label used in tables.

**Equivalence note.** Under this convention a path with a 50% TP1 fill is charged 12.5 bps on the entry notional, 6.25 bps on the TP1 notional and 6.25 bps on the final-exit notional, each at its own price.

**Change from DEV.** The DEV engine deducted one flat fraction of entry price per trade from the blended return (verified in the ledger: 0.0004 and 0.0025). That equals a round-trip charge on entry notional only, independent of exit price and ignoring the TP1 partial. DEV figures are restated under the per-fill convention before any comparison with new results, and both versions are shown once.

**Two-stage identity rule (new in v0.3) `[C05]`.** The frozen DEV record and the per-fill restatement are different versions of the numbers, and claims must not cross between them:

- **Stage 1 — original views.** The committed per-view ledgers (§3) are byte-identical evidence **under the original frozen flat per-trade convention**. Byte-identity and parity claims (P9) cover candidate/disposition identity fields and the values those fields carry under that convention.
- **Stage 2 — restatement.** The per-fill restatement is a **new version** of the ledgers. It **may alter net P&L** (different taken sets are possible, as the original views already demonstrate). It is committed as its own artifacts with its own hashes, and both versions are shown once, side by side.
- **Prohibition.** No document may claim unchanged P&L while changing the cost model, and no parity claim made at Stage 1 may be quoted as covering Stage 2 numbers.

**Fills.** Entries and market exits fill at the stated bar open or close with no partial-fill assumption. TP1 follows the base rule in section 7, stress-tested by E2. E1 stop fills carry 0.05 ATR of slippage on top of the cost model (a declared modelling assumption, §7).

**Not modeled.** Financing, borrow, dividends and taxes. Price adjustment for splits and dividends is audited in P10.

**H-BUY.** Costs cancel in the paired difference. The report adds a descriptive line, signal excess minus 25 bps, so readers can see whether the effect clears a cost.

**Sizing.** Trade-level tests are unaffected by sizing. Portfolio-level outputs use the sizing recorded under P6 and report peak gross notional against equity.

**Fixed in advance.** None of these values may change after any result is seen.

## 11. Decision rules

Each test returns PASS, FAIL or INCONCLUSIVE from its estimate θ̂, its one-sided 95% interval [L, U] and its minimum effect δ (§6). The rules are fixed before any untouched data is opened. This section is the protocol's **single** PASS/FAIL/INCONCLUSIVE contract; no other section may define a competing verdict.

| Verdict | Condition | Reported label |
|---|---|---|
| PASS | Validity floors and all section 3 and section 10 gates met, and L ≥ δ | CONFIRMED AT δ |
| FAIL | Floors and gates met, and U < δ. A useful-sized effect is excluded, including a real but too-small positive effect | EXCLUDED |
| INCONCLUSIVE | Everything else: floors or gates not met, contamination or protocol deviation, 0 < L < δ, or L ≤ 0 with U ≥ δ | POSITIVE, NOT CONFIRMED AT δ, or INDETERMINATE, plus UNDERPOWERED FOR PASS where section 6 applies it |

**Stages.**

- **Stage A:** one pooled analysis of Tier 2 plus Tier 3 on the holdout window, **once the audit is clean** — with the branch readings below for the other §9 outcomes `[C18]`.
- **Stage B:** one analysis of Tier 4 (forward, including forward new symbols), at the date fixed in section 10.
- "Edge supported" needs Stage B to PASS for H-SYS-1 and Stage A not to FAIL, because only the forward test has real fills and parity checks.
- "Edge rejected" if either stage FAILs H-SYS-1.
- Anything else is INCONCLUSIVE, and it is reported as that.

**Stage A under each §9 outcome `[C18]`.** Tier 3's eligibility is the same in every branch: confirmatory on the holdout window, supplementary on the DEV window. Because Tier 3's supplementary window shares market days with DEV, Tier 3-on-DEV results are never pooled with Tier 1 as if independent; all inference is over calendar-time blocks (§6), which carry every tier trading on the same days jointly, so shared-market-days covariance is inside the block design rather than assumed away.

| §9 outcome | Tier 2 in Stage A | Stage A composition | Reading of the edge rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Clean | Included, full confirmatory standing | Pooled Tier 2 + Tier 3 on the holdout window | As above: Stage A may PASS, FAIL or be INCONCLUSIVE on the pooled analysis |
| Partly contaminated | **Excluded from the confirmatory pool**; reported as supplementary with no PASS or FAIL standing | Tier 3 only, on the holdout window | Stage A returns no verdict on Tier 2's behalf. "Stage A not to FAIL" is read on the Tier-3-only analysis: the edge rule can still be satisfied (Stage B PASS + Tier-3 Stage A not FAIL), and the absence of Tier 2 standing is disclosed in every report of the verdict |
| Contaminated | **Excluded entirely**; Tier 2 retired, not reported as evidence | Tier 3 only, on the holdout window | As Partly contaminated, and confirmatory weight rests on Tiers 3 and 4 alone |

**Reading the three verdicts together.**

| H-SYS-1 | H-BUY-P | H-EXIT-3 | Reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| PASS | PASS | any | Edge supported; the signal contributes, and the exit result shows whether exits add to it |
| PASS | not PASS | any | Profit is not explained by signal quality; suspect concentration, luck or exit and portfolio effects; unresolved |
| not PASS | PASS | E0 not better than E3 | The signal may carry information that exits or costs consume; any redesign is a new version on fresh evidence |
| FAIL | FAIL | any | Rejected |

**No post-hoc changes.** δ, α, interval method, block length and cost model may not change after unblinding. A deviation is logged, and the verdict is INCONCLUSIVE unless Muse and the user declared the deviation immaterial before unblinding. Diagnostics in section 12 annotate a verdict and never flip it.
